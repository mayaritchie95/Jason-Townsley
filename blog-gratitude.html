From the Desk of a Psychotherapist: Cultivating Gratitude This Christmas — A Mindful CBT Perspective

The holiday season, while often joyous, is also a source of stress. Navigating family dynamics, managing expectations, and coping with financial pressures can leave individuals feeling overwhelmed and emotionally depleted. This year, consider incorporating a skill from Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) into your holiday toolkit: intentional gratitude.

CBT emphasizes the interconnectedness of our thoughts, feelings, and behaviours. By actively focusing on gratitude, we can influence our cognitive processes and promote emotional well-being. However, it’s crucial to understand that the focus of this gratitude must be genuinely meaningful to you. Vague or generalized affirmations, while well-intentioned, can sometimes backfire. If we force ourselves to express gratitude for things that don’t resonate with us on a deeper level, we may inadvertently dismiss or suppress emotions that require attention and processing.

Authentic gratitude stems from identifying and appreciating the things in our lives that truly hold value and significance. It’s about connecting with those experiences, relationships, or personal qualities that bring us joy, comfort, or a sense of purpose. This mindful approach allows us to cultivate a deeper sense of appreciation and reap the benefits of gratitude, including:

  • Reduced stress and anxiety: Shifting attention towards genuinely valued aspects of life can help counterbalance the anxieties often associated with the holiday season.
  • Improved mood and emotional regulation: Authentic gratitude practices can foster a sense of contentment and resilience in the face of challenges.
  • Enhanced interpersonal relationships: Expressing sincere appreciation for loved ones strengthens bonds and fosters a more positive social environment.

This holiday season, consider making gratitude a daily practice, but ensure that the focus is on what truly resonates with you. Just as we thoughtfully decorate our homes with cherished ornaments, we can also “decorate” our minds with thoughts of genuine appreciation. Here are a few practical strategies to cultivate meaningful gratitude:

  • Establish a daily gratitude practice: Take a few moments each morning or evening to reflect on aspects of your life that evoke a genuine sense of appreciation — personal attributes, relationships, experiences, or even simple pleasures that hold personal significance.
  • Practice specificity and authenticity: Delve into the specifics of what you are grateful for and why it holds meaning. For example, instead of “I am grateful for my health,” consider “I am grateful for the ability to enjoy a walk in the crisp winter air with my dog, which brings me a sense of peace and connection.”
  • Maintain a gratitude journal: Documenting experiences of gratitude in a journal can serve as a tangible reminder of the positive aspects of your life. Reflect on why these experiences are meaningful to you and how they contribute to your well-being.
  • Attach the practice to an everyday behaviour: The likelihood of behaviour change increases if you attach the new behaviour to something you already do. For example, practice your intentional gratitude as you commute, as you brush your teeth, as you shower, or as you lay your head on the pillow at night (my personal go-to).

While the holidays may bring their share of challenges, incorporating intentional and meaningful gratitude into your daily routine can help foster a sense of peace, joy, and connection. Important disclaimer to manage our expectations: while this practice helps, it’s but one part of a broader depth and breadth of mental wellness skills.

Wishing you a season filled with peace, joy, and intentional gratitude.
